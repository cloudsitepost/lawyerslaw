Social Security Attorney: Everything You
Need To Know Before Hiring A Lawyer


Navigating the process of applying for Social Security benefits can be quite challenging, particularly if your application is rejected, your health condition impacts your job capability, or if you're uncertain about your eligibility status. Familiarizing yourself with Social Security regulations, collecting the requisite documentation, and adhering to crucial deadlines can feel daunting without expert guidance.

A Social Security attorney can clarify your rights, assist with your application, and guide you through the appeals process. Whether you’re seeking Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) or Supplemental Security Income (SSI), working with a knowledgeable lawyer can enhance your understanding of the legal criteria and help you steer clear of frequent pitfalls.


What Is a Social Security Attorney?


A Social Security attorney is a legal expert who assists people in navigating the process of applying for, appealing, and securing benefits from the Social Security Administration (SSA). They are well-versed in federal regulations, criteria for eligibility, standards for medical evidence, and the procedures for administrative hearings.

Their main role is to support clients throughout the claims process while safeguarding their legal rights. These attorneys may work with those who have disabilities, clients whose claims have been rejected, and individuals needing legal representation in hearings.





Types of Social Security Benefits

  • Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI): This program offers financial support to those who meet the criteria for disabilities and have earned enough work credits.

  • Supplemental Security Income (SSI): This initiative provides monetary aid to individuals who are elderly, visually impaired, or disabled, provided they fall within the specified income and resource guidelines.

  • Survivor Benefits: This program informs qualifying family members about their entitlements after the loss of a wage earner.

  • Retirement Benefits: This service helps resolve conflicts related to eligibility, calculation of benefits, or any decisions made by Social Security.

Be aware that not all attorneys are equipped to handle every claim type, so it’s important to verify that the lawyer has relevant experience tailored to your unique circumstances.


When Should You Hire a Social Security Lawyer?


Engaging a Social Security attorney can be particularly beneficial if your case involves intricate medical details, questions about eligibility, or if your application has been rejected. 

  • Your initial application for disability has been turned down.

  • You need to file for reconsideration or request a hearing.

  • Your medical documentation fails to clearly illustrate how your condition affects your work capabilities.

  • You struggle to comprehend SSA communications, including deadlines and necessary documentation.

  • You suspect that the SSA made a mistake in processing your claim or in calculating your benefits.

A lawyer can clarify your options, pinpoint issues in your application, and guide you on the best course of action.


What Services Does a Social Security Attorney Provide?

  • Application Assembly and Evidence Gathering: Your lawyer can examine your medical documents, work history, treatment details, and additional necessary paperwork.

  • Appeal Process and Hearing Support: Should your application be rejected, an attorney can clarify the grounds for the denial and assist you in filing an appeal.

  • Interaction with the SSA: A lawyer can liaise with the SSA for you, keep track of your case progress, and aid in addressing any information requests. 




How Much Does a Social Security Attorney Cost?

Numerous attorneys specializing in Social Security disability cases operate on a contingency fee structure, which means they are paid only if you are awarded benefits. According to the customary fee agreement guidelines set by the SSA, the allowable fee is typically capped at either 25% of your back benefits or a predetermined maximum amount, depending on which is the lesser figure. It's important to note that this maximum can vary, and alternative fee arrangements might have different approval criteria.

Before you decide to hire a lawyer, inquire about their fee structure, any potential costs, and whether you might be responsible for expenses if your claim is not approved. 


How to Choose the Right Social Security Attorney

It’s essential to find someone with pertinent experience, effective communication skills, and a solid grasp of Social Security protocols.

  • Experience: Inquire about the attorney’s history with cases like yours.

  • Communication: Clarify how you can reach the lawyer and how updates will be provided.

  • Reputation: Look into independent reviews from clients and their professional qualifications.

  • Fee transparency: Ensure that the lawyer clearly outlines their fees and any other charges.

  • Case strategy: Discuss the approach the attorney intends to take regarding your specific circumstances.

Lastly, verify that the lawyer holds the appropriate licenses to represent clients before the Social Security Administration (SSA).
