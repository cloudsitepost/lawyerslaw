How To Analyze DMARC Reports

    For Better Domain Protection

    Email security is essential for protecting businesses against phishing, email spoofing, and domain impersonation. Attackers often exploit trusted domain names to send fraudulent emails, steal sensitive information, or deceive customers. Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance (DMARC) helps organizations reduce these risks by combining email authentication with reporting and enforcement policies.

    However, implementing DMARC is only the first step. Understanding its reports is equally important for identifying unauthorized senders, resolving authentication failures, and improving domain protection. By analyzing DMARC reports regularly, businesses can monitor email activity, strengthen authentication, and maintain reliable email delivery.


    Understanding DMARC Reports and Their Purpose


    DMARC reports provide information about how receiving mail servers evaluate messages sent using your domain. They help administrators determine whether messages pass authentication checks and whether suspicious sources are attempting to impersonate their organization.


    Aggregate Reports (RUA)

    Aggregate reports summarize email authentication activity over a specific reporting period. They typically include sending IP addresses, message volumes, SPF and DKIM results, and the DMARC policy applied to messages.

    These reports are particularly useful for identifying legitimate email services, detecting unfamiliar sending sources, and monitoring authentication performance across multiple providers.




    Forensic Reports (RUF)

    Forensic reports can provide more detailed information about individual messages that fail DMARC evaluation. However, not all receiving providers send these reports, and the available information varies. Because they may contain sensitive email data, organizations should consider privacy implications before requesting or processing them.

    For most businesses, aggregate reports provide the primary foundation for routine DMARC monitoring.


    Steps to Analyze DMARC Reports Effectively


    A consistent analysis process helps turn raw reporting data into practical security improvements.


    Step 1: Identify All Sending Sources

    Start by reviewing the IP addresses and sending services listed in your aggregate reports. Compare these sources with your approved email infrastructure, including Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, marketing platforms, CRM systems, and transactional email providers.

    Investigate unfamiliar IP addresses before taking action. An unknown source may indicate unauthorized activity, but it could also belong to a legitimate third-party service that has not been documented.

    Maintaining an inventory of authorized senders makes it easier to identify potential spoofing attempts and prevent legitimate messages from being mistakenly rejected.


    Step 2: Examine SPF and DKIM Authentication

    Next, review SPF and DKIM results to understand why messages pass or fail DMARC.

    SPF (Sender Policy Framework) checks whether the sending IP address is authorized by the domain's SPF record. DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) verifies a cryptographic signature associated with the message.

    DMARC requires a passing SPF or DKIM result that also aligns with the domain shown in the visible From address. If both mechanisms fail to meet the required authentication and alignment conditions, the message fails DMARC.

    Look for repeated failures from legitimate services. Correcting missing SPF authorizations, DKIM signing problems, or domain alignment issues can improve authentication without disrupting normal email operations.




    Step 3: Review DMARC Policy Results

    Examine how receiving servers handle messages under your current DMARC policy. The three primary policies are:

    • p=none: Monitors authentication results without requesting DMARC-based rejection or quarantine.

    • p=quarantine: Requests that failing messages be treated as suspicious, often placed in spam.

    • p=reject: Requests that failing messages be rejected.

    Use reporting data to confirm that legitimate email sources authenticate correctly before moving toward stricter enforcement. A rushed policy change can affect genuine business communications.


    Detect Threats and Improve Domain Protection


    DMARC reports are valuable for identifying suspicious patterns that might otherwise go unnoticed.


    Investigate Unusual Sending Activity

    Pay attention to unexpected IP addresses, sudden increases in message volume, repeated authentication failures, and sources claiming to represent your domain without authorization. Compare suspicious activity across reporting periods to distinguish isolated incidents from recurring patterns.

    If an unauthorized source appears, investigate the origin and determine whether it represents spoofing, a misconfigured service, or a compromised legitimate system. Update authentication records and security controls when necessary.


    Use DMARC Analysis Tools

    Manual report analysis can become difficult when a domain receives thousands of emails daily. Tools such as AutoSPF, PowerDMARC, EasyDMARC, and MXToolbox can help administrators inspect authentication configurations or interpret relevant reporting data.
