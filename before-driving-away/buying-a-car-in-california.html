California Car Buyers' Checklist:
Everything To Do Before Driving Away

Acquiring a vehicle in California is a thrilling achievement, whether you're opting for a new car or a pre-owned model. However, prior to leaving the dealership, it's crucial to be aware of your obligations, carefully examine the paperwork, and ensure the car aligns with your desires. Ignoring key aspects could result in unforeseen costs, issues with registration, or conflicts with the seller.

From assessing the car's condition to verifying your insurance, here’s a complete list of what to review before you drive off with your new vehicle in California.


1. Research the Vehicle Before Making a Purchase


Compare Prices and Vehicle History

Begin by investigating the market value, dependability, upkeep expenses, and safety assessments of the vehicle. For a used car purchase, acquire a vehicle history report to uncover any prior accidents, title problems, odometer tampering, or reported damages. 

Evaluate pricing from various dealerships and private sellers to see if the price is fair. 


Check for Recalls and Mechanical Problems

Check for any active safety recalls by accessing the recall database of the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration. If you're buying a pre-owned vehicle, it's advisable to schedule an inspection by a certified mechanic prior to completing the transaction.





2. Inspect and Test-Drive the Car


Examine the Vehicle's Condition

Examine the outside of the vehicle for any marks, dents, rust, or uneven paint, as these might suggest prior repairs. Assess the condition of the tires, headlights, taillights, windows, and interior elements.

When evaluating pre-owned vehicles, keep an eye out for warning indicators, strange sounds from the engine, fluid leaks, and indications of water damage. If possible, ask for the vehicle's maintenance history.


Take a Thorough Test Drive

If you can, take the car on various road surfaces. Assess the brakes, steering, acceleration, transmission, air conditioning, and safety systems. Ensure you feel at ease while driving and that all components function properly. 

Don’t rush into buying a vehicle; allow yourself ample time to thoroughly assess it first.


3. Review the Purchase Agreement Carefully


Verify the Total Cost

Prior to signing, take the time to examine the purchase agreement carefully, ensuring you verify the vehicle's cost, as well as all applicable taxes, registration fees, dealer-installed options, and additional charges. If you're opting for financing, be sure to assess the annual percentage rate (APR), duration of the loan, monthly installment, and overall payable amount. 

It's important to ensure that any commitments made verbally by the salesperson are recorded in written form.


Understand Your Cancellation Rights

California typically does not have a standard three-day grace period for canceling vehicle purchases. However, certain buyers of used cars may be eligible for a cancellation option offered by the dealer, which comes with specific requirements and fees. 


4. Confirm Warranty Coverage and Consumer Protections


Understand the Warranty Terms

Check if the vehicle is backed by a manufacturer's warranty, a dealer warranty, or an extended service plan. Examine details such as coverage limits, exclusions, repair processes, and the warranty duration.

California lemon law may provide additional remedies for defective vehicles with ongoing issues that affect their use, value, or safety.

Keep All Relevant Documents

Keep the purchase contract, warranty information, financing documents, inspection findings, and any written descriptions regarding the vehicle's state. These records may prove important should any disagreements occur in the future.





5. Arrange Insurance and Vehicle Registration


Verify Insurance Coverage

Prior to hitting the road, make sure your car insurance complies with California's legal standards. Reach out to your insurance provider to either include the vehicle in your policy or secure the necessary coverage, and ensure you receive confirmation that the coverage is active.


Check Registration and Title Documents

Verify that the dealership will take care of the necessary title and registration documents, if needed. For purchases made from private sellers, be aware of your obligations and timelines for transferring ownership and registering the vehicle with the California Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV). 


6. Complete a Final Walk-Around


Before departing, ensure you have all keys, remotes, manuals, and any promised accessories. Confirm that the vehicle identification number (VIN) aligns with the purchase paperwork, and check for any new warning lights or unexpected problems. 

Capture photographs of the vehicle's state and familiarize yourself with how to use key features.
