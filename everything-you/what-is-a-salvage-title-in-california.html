What Is A Salvage Title In California?
Everything You Need To Know

When buying or selling a used vehicle in California, it’s important to understand its title status. A salvage title means the vehicle has sustained significant damage or an insurance company has declared it a total loss. It may still be repairable and roadworthy, but it can involve additional legal, safety, insurance, and registration requirements. Knowing what a salvage title means can help you make an informed decision about buying a damaged vehicle or selling one after a serious accident. 


What Is a Salvage Title in California?


In California, a salvage title identifies a vehicle that has been declared a total loss or otherwise meets the state’s salvage classification requirements. This often follows an accident, flood, fire, theft, or other major incident when repairs are economically impractical. Insurers commonly declare a vehicle a total loss when repair costs approach or exceed its actual cash value, though California law allows for other circumstances that can result in salvage status. 

In California, a salvage title marks a vehicle that an insurer has declared a total loss because of significant damage, theft, or another qualifying incident. The title signals a history of substantial damage; repairs and a state inspection may be required before the vehicle can be legally driven again.




Common Reasons a Vehicle Gets a Salvage Title


Several types of damage or circumstances may lead to a vehicle receiving a salvage designation in California:

  • Accident damage: A serious collision that damages the frame, engine, suspension, or other major components may result in a total-loss declaration.

  • Flood damage: Significant water exposure may cause electrical problems, corrosion, or mechanical failures.

  • Fire damage: Extensive fire damage may affect the engine, wiring, interior, or structural components.

  • Theft recovery: Depending on the circumstances and applicable reporting requirements, a recovered stolen vehicle may qualify for salvage classification.

  • Natural disasters: Damage from storms, falling objects, or other disasters may also lead to salvage status.


Difference Between Salvage Title and Revived Salvage Title

California distinguishes between a vehicle declared a total loss and one that has been repaired and approved for road use.

A Salvage Certificate identifies a vehicle declared a total loss. It generally means the vehicle is not currently cleared for ordinary road use.

A revived salvage vehicle was previously declared a total loss but has since been repaired, inspected, and registered for road use. It can be driven legally once it meets applicable DMV registration and safety requirements. 


How to Register a Salvage Vehicle in California


To drive a repaired salvage vehicle legally in California, complete the required registration steps:

  • Repair the vehicle: Restore it to safe operating condition with appropriate replacement parts and professional repairs when needed.

  • Gather your documents: Prepare the Application for Title or Registration (REG 343), proof of ownership, and any applicable salvage documents.

  • Complete vehicle verification: Obtain verification using form REG 31 or, when required, a California Highway Patrol (CHP) inspection certificate.

  • Pass the safety inspection: Obtain an electronic Vehicle Safety Systems Inspection (VSSI) certificate.

  • Apply for registration: Submit the required documents, applicable fees, and any additional certifications to the California DMV.

Depending on the vehicle and its registration circumstances, you may also need a smog certification. Requirements can vary based on the vehicle’s history.



Can You Buy a Car With a Salvage Title in California?

Yes. California consumers can buy salvage-title vehicles, but it’s important to assess the vehicle carefully before purchasing.

  • Get a vehicle history report to review past accidents and title records.

  • Have an independent mechanic inspect its mechanical and structural condition.

  • Review repair invoices and documentation for replacement parts.

  • Confirm whether the vehicle can be insured and registered.

  • Compare its price with similar clean-title vehicles.

Insurance companies may offer limited coverage or different terms for salvage vehicles, so confirm what coverage is available before you buy.


How Does a Salvage Title Affect a Vehicle's Value?

A salvage title can significantly reduce a vehicle’s resale value because buyers may be concerned about previous damage, repair quality, and potential safety issues. Even after repairs, a vehicle with a revived salvage title is generally worth less than a comparable vehicle with a clean title.

It may also be harder to insure, finance, or resell a salvage-title vehicle. Some lenders restrict loans for branded-title vehicles, while buyers may hesitate because of the vehicle’s damage history.
